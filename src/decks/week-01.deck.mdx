# What Wins a Game?

If destroying the Nexus is the only objective, why do players spend most of
the game doing something else?

---

## An objective you can't aim at

The Nexus is the only structure whose destruction ends the game.

For almost the entire match, a direct attempt on it is also a bad decision —
towers and inhibitors see to that.

So players spend the game doing something else. This course asks what that
something else is for.

```comment
Land the paradox before naming the chain: the win condition is the one thing
you can't act on for most of the game.
```

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## The chain

```text
resources  →  advantages  →  control  →  objectives  →  Nexus
```

Gold, experience, wards, cooldowns → a lead → what that lead buys on the map
→ Dragon, Baron, towers → the only structure that ends the game.

Every arrow is a conversion. Every conversion can fail.

---

## None of it is the goal

- **Kills** are a resource transfer plus a control window — the dead
  player's absence. The kill buys nothing on its own.
- **Gold** is spending power, not yet an advantage until it's spent.
- **Dragon** turns earlier map control into a stat buff.
- **Baron** forces a fight or a siege window; the icon is not the mechanism.

```comment
Four short beats, not a lecture within the slide — the point is that each
noun students already track is a means, not an end.
```

---

{/* _class: quote */}

## The principle

> Every advantage must eventually be converted.

Gold, kills, vision, tempo and objectives matter for exactly one reason:
what they let a team do next.

---

## A ten-kill lead that is losing

Twenty minutes in: blue leads ten kills to two.

Read the rest of the board. Every kill was an isolated 2-for-1, no towers
are down, there's no vision near Baron — and quiet red has a stacked
Dragon soul and full jungle camps.

Ahead in kills is not the same claim as ahead in the game.

---

## Why did they win?

Think of a match you've played or watched recently. Answer, in whatever
language you currently have:

**Why did the winning team actually win?**

"They got more kills" is a fine starting answer today. Write it down —
Week 12 asks the same question again.
