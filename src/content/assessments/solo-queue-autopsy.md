## The brief

> Analyse one of your own losses.

Pick a game you lost — recently enough that you remember it in detail, and
one where you were not simply outclassed from minute one. Identify **three
decisions** that materially changed the outcome, and explain each one using
the vocabulary from Weeks 1–4: what resources or power were at stake, what
wave state the decision was made in or created, what the trade actually
cost, and — most importantly — what it changed about the decisions available
afterward.

A decision here means a choice someone had available, not a mechanical
error. "I missed a skillshot" is not a decision. "I chose to all-in on a
slow-pushed wave with my flash down" is.

**Another player's mistake may only be discussed in terms of how it changed
the decisions available to you.** You are not writing about what your
jungler should have done differently. You are writing about what their
mistake meant for what you could then do.

## What you submit

A written analysis, structured around your three decisions, one per section:

1. **The moment** — when it happened (an in-game timestamp is enough) and
   the game state at that point: gold and level, wave state, cooldowns,
   whatever's relevant.
2. **The decision** — what was chosen, and what the alternative was.
3. **The conversion (or the failure to convert)** — what the decision
   changed about what either team could do next, using Week 1's chain,
   Week 2's power, Week 3's wave, or Week 4's trade as the explanatory tool.

Close with one sentence per decision distinguishing outcome from decision
quality: was this a good decision that didn't pay off, or a bad decision
that happened not to matter yet?

## Marking

|Criterion|Weight|
|---|---|
|Decision identification|25%|
|Evidence from game state|20%|
|Use of course concepts|25%|
|Causal reasoning|20%|
|Outcome vs decision quality|10%|

**Decision identification** rewards choosing decisions that actually shaped
the game over the three biggest mechanical errors — a clean, well-timed
decision that turned out to matter counts; a missed combo does not.

**Evidence from game state** rewards a specific, checkable moment for each
decision over a general impression of "we were losing by then."

**Use of course concepts** rewards explanations built from resources,
power, the wave, or trading, over generic commentary ("bad decision,"
"should have known better") that doesn't name what actually changed.

**Causal reasoning** rewards tracing what a decision changed about the
decisions available afterward, not just narrating what happened next.

**Outcome vs decision quality** rewards telling the two apart. Rank,
blame, and hindsight ("obviously that was going to go badly") earn nothing
on their own.
