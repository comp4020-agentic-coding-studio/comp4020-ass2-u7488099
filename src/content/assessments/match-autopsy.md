## The brief

> Explain why one team won a complete match, using concepts from across the
> semester.

This is the course's culminating assessment. You will be given a complete
match to analyse. The task is not to describe what happened — it's to
explain why it happened, the way Week 12 asked you to trace a match you
already knew.

## What you submit

Identify a **limited number of pivotal transitions** — moments where the
game state genuinely changed, not every event in the match. For each one,
state:

1. **The game state beforehand** — what was true before this moment.
2. **The decision or event that changed it.**
3. **The advantage that resulted.**
4. **How that advantage was converted** — or, if it wasn't, why not.
5. **What became available or unavailable afterward**, for either team.
6. **How this contributed to the eventual win.**

Draw on evidence from across the semester — resources, wave states,
information, tempo, objectives, composition and win conditions,
teamfighting — wherever a transition actually calls for it. You do not
need to name-drop every course concept; a transition that's fully
explained by two or three of them does not need the rest forced in.

**A coherent causal argument is worth more than vocabulary density.** An
explanation that correctly uses fewer concepts, tied tightly to evidence,
outperforms one that mentions everything from the semester without
connecting the mentions into a chain.

## What the criteria reward

**Identification of pivotal transitions** rewards choosing moments that
actually changed the game's trajectory, over an exhaustive or arbitrary
list of events.

**Causal reasoning per transition** is weighted heaviest: for each
transition, does the explanation trace state → change → advantage →
conversion → what it opened or closed, or does it stop at narrating what
happened?

**Evidence from the match** rewards specific, checkable moments over
general impressions of momentum.

**Breadth across course concepts** rewards drawing on multiple areas of
the course where the match genuinely calls for them — not a checklist of
every week's vocabulary regardless of relevance.

**Coherence of the overall argument** rewards an explanation that reads as
one connected causal chain from draft to Nexus, over a series of
disconnected correct observations. This is the assessment where the
distance travelled since Week 1 should be obvious.
