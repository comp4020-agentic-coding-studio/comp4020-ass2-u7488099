## Guiding question

> What is an advantage actually worth?

## A gold count is a rear-view mirror

A player's gold total tells you what they have earned, not what they can
currently do. Combat power at any given moment depends on what's already
been spent — items completed, boots upgraded, a skill point put into the
ability that matters right now — not on what's sitting unspent in the bank.
Two players can carry the same number, and be in entirely different games.

## Same gold, different game

Both players below have 3,200 gold at the fourteen-minute mark.

| Metric | Player A | Player B |
|---|---|---|
| Level | 9 | 8 |
| Item state | first item complete, boots upgraded | two items started, neither complete, no boots upgrade |
| Ultimate | off cooldown | on cooldown from a trade four minutes ago |
| What they can do right now | all-in a trade and expect to win it | survive a trade, not start one |

Nominal gold: identical. Actual capability: not close. Power arrives at
spikes — an item completion, a level threshold that unlocks or upgrades an
ability — not as a smooth curve matched to gold earned. A gold lead that
hasn't crossed the next spike yet is a lead in name only.

## Levels are lumpy too

Experience works the same way. The stat gains from levelling are continuous,
but the decisions they unlock are not: level 6 is when most champions first
have their ultimate, and level 11 or 16 is often a second or third rank in
it. An experience lead that closes the gap to one of those thresholds first
is worth far more than the same lead measured at a level where nothing
changes.

## Denial: making the same lead worth less

An advantage's value isn't fixed once it's earned — the other side gets a
say in how much of it survives contact. Killing a fed opponent grants a
shutdown, bonus gold sized to how far ahead they were, which claws back
part of what they'd built. Zoning a lane opponent off minions denies
experience and gold without a single item being bought. Even without a kill,
manipulating the wave (Week 3) to control who can safely last-hit reduces
how much of an existing gold lead the other side can keep converting. Worth
is not just what an advantage is when it's earned; it's what's left of it by
the time someone tries to spend it.

## Builds on Week 1

Week 1's chain was resources → advantages → control. This week makes the
first arrow concrete: gold and experience (resources) only become an
advantage once they cross an item completion or a level threshold — the
exact moment a number on a scoreboard turns into an option a champion
didn't have a minute ago.
