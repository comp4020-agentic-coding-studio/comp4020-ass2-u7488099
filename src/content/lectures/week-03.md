## Guiding question

> Why do six minions determine what ten champions are allowed to do?

## A resource with a mind of its own

Every other resource this course discusses — gold, experience, cooldowns —
belongs to a player and moves when they do. The minion wave belongs to
neither laner, has its own rules for where it sits, and those rules
determine what both players are allowed to do while it's between them. A
laner ahead in gold and level (Week 2) still can't act on that lead if the
wave has them pinned under their own tower.

## Four wave states

- **Neutral / even** — the wave meets near the midpoint, roughly matched
  kills on both sides. Neither laner is restricted; both can freely roam,
  recall, or fight without giving up much.
- **Frozen** — the wave is held just outside the tower of one laner, kept
  there deliberately by the other. The laner behind the freeze can farm
  safely but can't extend forward without walking into the other's minions
  and, often, a jungler.
- **Slow push** — one side stops last-hitting, minions stack up unmatched,
  and the wave grows larger while it slowly moves toward the other tower.
- **Crashing** — the stacked wave finally reaches a tower and clears into
  it fast, opening a short window where nobody needs to be in the lane at
  all.

This week's deck walks each of those states with a diagram, plus what
happens at the moment one state turns into the next — the bounce back off a
tower, the moment a freeze breaks.

## What a state buys you

The reason a lane opponent's wave state matters more than their gold total
is that it gates access to everything off to the side: whether a recall is
safe (crashing a wave buys the time to shop and return before it matters),
whether a roam is punished (a slow push left alone gets crashed onto a
tower, or worse, invites a gank the other way), and whether contesting an
objective is even an option (a frozen wave means one laner cannot leave to
help, whatever their gold total says they could otherwise do).

## Builds on Weeks 1–2

The wave is simultaneously a resource, in Week 1's sense — something both
sides draw power from — and a constraint on time and space that decides
whether the power from Week 2 can actually be spent this minute. A gold lead
that can't leave its own tower because of the wave is exactly the "advantage
that hasn't been converted" from Week 1's principle, with a cause you can
now name.
