## Guiding question

> Why can recalling be more valuable than getting another kill?

## Tempo, defined

Tempo is not momentum, and it isn't a vibe read on who's "winning." It's the
relationship between what a player can do right now and how much time doing
it costs before they get to do the next thing. Every window — a recall, a
gank, an objective contest — either gets spent on the highest-value action
available in it, or it doesn't, and there's no getting it back afterward.

## The vocabulary

- **turns / windows to act** — a stretch of time in which a specific action
  is available and reasonably safe to take
- **recalls and resets** — deliberately spending a window on returning to
  base rather than pressing an advantage further
- **synchronised resets** — teammates timing recalls together so the whole
  team returns to the map itemised and levelled at the same moment, instead
  of trickling back one at a time
- **rotations** — moving from one part of the map to another, spending
  travel time against whatever's gained on arrival
- **initiative** — having the first credible move available in a given
  window, which is frequently worth more than having the stronger move
- **opportunity cost** — what didn't get done because the window was spent
  on something else
- **arriving first** — the practical shape initiative takes: the same fight
  or objective is a different proposition for whoever's already there

## A kill versus a reset, sixty seconds later

| Time | Team A (stays) | Team B (resets) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Wins a skirmish bot lane: one kill, a minor gold lead | Loses the skirmish; player is down but alive |
| 0:10 | Pushes the advantage — chases the wave in, looks for a second pick | Recalls immediately with whatever gold is banked |
| 0:45 | Still contesting bot lane, chasing marginal value from the same fight | Returns fully itemised: item completions and a boots upgrade the earlier gold bought |
| 1:15 | Finally recalls, late, still mid-return when the next window opens | Already back on the map, positioned near the next contestable objective |
| 1:40 | Arrives under-itemised, mid-recall or freshly returned | Arrives first, fully itemised — takes the window uncontested |

Team A "won" the skirmish. Team B's reset converted the same sixty seconds
into arriving first and stronger at the next window that actually mattered.
The kill bought a moment; the reset bought the next window.

## Every advantage must eventually be converted

That kill only means something if it's spent on something — another
objective, more map pressure, a bigger fight. Staying to chase marginal
value from an already-won skirmish is not converting the advantage; it's
delaying the reset that would let the team spend it somewhere the enemy
can't answer. A kill followed by a late or absent reset can end up worth
less than it looked in the moment it happened.

## This is not "recall good"

The point isn't that resetting is always correct. A reset taken while
already ahead, safe, and positioned for the next window wastes exactly the
same tempo the other way — the team gives up initiative it already had for
no reason. The claim is narrower and less catchy: match the action to the
window that's actually open, and notice when staying for one more play is
quietly spending tempo the team will need in ninety seconds.

## Builds on Weeks 1–5

Week 2 established that power arrives in spikes, not smoothly — tempo is
what decides whether a team reaches the next spike before or after the
opponent does. Week 5's jungler is constantly trading tempo for
information: every camp cleared or gank attempted is time not spent
elsewhere, gambled against what tracking (or being tracked) is worth.
