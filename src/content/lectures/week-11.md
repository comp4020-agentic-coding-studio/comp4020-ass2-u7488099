## Guiding question

> If the rules barely change, why does the correct way to play change
> constantly?

## The meta is an answer, not a list

"The meta" is not a list of currently-correct champions issued somewhere.
It's an emergent answer to a moving environment: patches shift small
numbers, players discover interactions nobody drafted around, and once
enough people copy a strategy that works, its very popularity becomes part
of the environment the next strategy has to answer. None of this requires
the game's rules to have changed at all.

## What actually moves

- **patches and balance changes** — the most visible driver, but far from
  the only one
- **player discovery** — a use for an ability, a timing, or a composition
  interaction nobody had tried at this level of optimisation before
- **optimisation** — the same tools, played with steadily fewer mistakes,
  which itself changes what a plan can rely on
- **professional play versus solo queue** — coordinated five-player
  execution unlocks strategies that fall apart without it, and vice versa
- **copying without understanding** — a strategy spreads faster than the
  reasons it works, and gets applied in games where those reasons don't
  hold
- **adaptation** — every strategy above eventually gets an answer, once
  enough opponents see it often enough to prepare one

## A strategy becomes popular: a case

Suppose teams start grouping all five players near an early objective
before it spawns, abandoning normal lane assignments to do it. Distinguish
four things about this:

**The observable behaviour.** Five players in one river area in the first
few minutes, lanes briefly empty.

**The reason it works.** When a coordinated early group can force a
five-versus-five fight (or an uncontested take) worth more, in expected
value, than five separate lanes' worth of farm — because the objective's
reward and the value of removing an opponent early both outweigh the
minions given up — grouping is the stronger play, not a gimmick.

**The conditions supporting it.** This only holds while the objective is
valuable enough relative to lost farm, and while the popular champion pool
has strong early skirmish tools rather than tools that need uninterrupted
lane time to matter. Change either condition and the arithmetic changes.

**What could make it stop working.** A balance change that shrinks the
objective's value, an opposing draft that punishes grouping with side-lane
pressure (Week 9) the group can't answer, or simply enough teams learning
to contest the group evenly instead of conceding it.

## Builds on Weeks 9 and 10

The draft-level bets from Week 9 are exactly what shift when the meta
shifts — a comp built to force early fights is stronger when the popular
answer to it hasn't been worked out yet, and weaker once it has. Week 10's
fights are where those shifts get tested against a live opponent, which is
also how a strategy's weaknesses get discovered in the first place.

## A real limitation

Understanding why something worked is not the same as proving it will
always work. A causal story that explains one team's win — the kind Week
12 asks you to build — is not a law. Opponents adapt specifically to
strategies that beat them, balance changes remove the conditions a
strategy depended on, and a correct read of one game's environment can be
a wrong read of the next one. This is the same reason this course avoids
absolute rules like "always take Dragon": conditions, not commandments,
are what strategic correctness answers to.
