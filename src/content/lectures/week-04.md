## Guiding question

> What are you really trading when you fight another champion?

## A trade has more line items than a scoreboard

"Who won that trade" usually gets answered by comparing health bars, which
is the least informative comparison available. A trade also exchanges:

- **health** — the obvious one, and the least of it
- **mana or resource** — spent abilities that need time or a recall to
  refill
- **cooldowns** — an ability used is an ability unavailable the next time
  it's needed
- **summoner spells** — a Flash or Heal burned is a missing answer to the
  next threat, on both sides
- **position** — where each champion ends up relative to their tower, their
  jungler, and the wave
- **CS and wave control** — time spent trading is time not spent
  last-hitting, and it changes which of Week 3's wave states the lane ends
  up in
- **threat** — what each side can credibly do to the other immediately
  afterward
- **recall timing** — whether either side can safely leave the lane to
  spend what they just gained

## Winning the trade, losing the exchange

A laner lands a strong combo, takes their opponent to a sliver of health,
and by the health-bar measure, wins clearly. But landing it burned their own
mana and their engage ability's cooldown, and it happened on a wave that
was already slow-pushing toward the enemy tower. Their opponent recalls,
buys back health and items, and returns to a lane where the pushed wave is
now crashing into that same laner's own tower — unaccompanied, mana-low,
and without the ability that made the earlier trade work — right as the
enemy jungler arrives. The HP trade was won. The exchange was not: every
other line item above ended up on the other side of the ledger.

## Builds on Weeks 2–3

A trade's actual stakes are Week 2's power (whose cooldowns and item state
let them keep threatening after the exchange) and Week 3's wave (what
position the fight leaves the lane in, and who that position benefits).
Reading a trade by HP alone throws both away.

## Looking ahead: Solo Queue Autopsy

This is the vocabulary Assessment 1 asks you to use on your own games: not
"I won that fight" or "I lost that fight," but what the fight actually
traded, and what it changed about what either side could do next.
