## Guiding question

> What does your team need to happen in order to win?

## Draft sets the constraints before the game does

Every match starts with both teams already unequal in one respect: what
each composition's tools are good at. This isn't a tier list judgment —
strong individual champions still lose to a plan that denies them the game
state they need. Draft is where each team acquires a different set of
possibilities, and a different set of constraints, before a single minion
has spawned.

## Win condition, defined carefully

A win condition is not a single instruction like "get five kills" or
"reach 40 minutes." It means: **the kinds of game states that make this
team's tools more valuable than the opponent's.** A composition built
around forcing early fights has a win condition that names an early,
skirmish-heavy game — not a guaranteed early win, just a state where its
tools outperform the alternative. The same composition in a slow, static
lane-swap game may be worth very little, through no fault of the players.

## The vocabulary

- **engage** — tools that start a fight on the team's own terms
- **disengage / peel** — tools that end a fight early or protect a
  specific target inside it
- **poke** — damage applied at a range the opponent can't immediately
  answer, worn down rather than delivered in one commitment
- **front-to-back fighting** — a team's frontline absorbs and threatens
  while its backline deals damage from behind it
- **pick potential** — the ability to isolate and remove a single enemy
  before a full fight starts
- **side-lane pressure** — a composition that punishes a team for grouping,
  by threatening towers elsewhere
- **range, scaling, damage profile** — what a champion or comp can
  threaten now versus what it can threaten once it's ahead
- **access to targets** — whether a team's damage can actually reach the
  target it needs to remove, or whether the opponent can prevent that
- **composition interaction** — how one team's tools specifically answer,
  or fail to answer, the other's

## A concrete draft case

**Team A** drafts around forcing fights early: a hard-engage jungler, a
dive-capable top laner, a mobile burst mage in the mid lane, and a support
built for enabling all-ins. Its hyper-carry marksman is immobile and needs
protection, but is the strongest thing in the game once fully itemised.

**Team B** drafts to survive to that point: a disengage-capable top laner,
a jungler who paths for vision and counter-engage rather than ganks, a
long-range poke mage, and a support built around shields and knockbacks.
Its marksman scales at least as hard as Team A's, and has enough mobility
to reposition out of a bad engage.

Before watching the game, answer:

1. **What does Team A want the game to look like?** Early skirmishes,
   picks before either team's second item spike, forced fights at choke
   points where its engage can land cleanly.
2. **What does Team B want the game to look like?** Clean, uncontested
   lanes, poke that whittles a target down before any commitment, a game
   that reaches the point where both marksmen are core-item complete.
3. **Where do those plans conflict?** Directly, in the mid-game window
   around two completed items — Team A's win condition expires if it
   can't force a decisive fight before that point; Team B's win condition
   only exists if it survives to it.
4. **What evidence during the game would tell us one plan is succeeding?**
   For Team A: an early kill lead, tower plates down before fifteen
   minutes, its jungler present and active in the bot lane. For Team B:
   clean, uncontested farm, item timers on schedule, and — tellingly — Team
   A's jungler spending cooldowns and summoner spells on ganks that don't
   convert into anything.

## Builds on Weeks 1 and 4

Week 1's chain (resources → advantages → control → objectives → Nexus)
still applies to a draft: a composition is a bet about *which* resources
it can convert fastest, and into what. Week 4's trading vocabulary is what
"forcing a fight" or "surviving to scale" actually cashes out as, moment to
moment, once the game starts.

## What draft actually decides

Draft doesn't just pick which champions are in the game — it decides
which resources matter more to each side, which of Week 3's wave states
each team wants to create, where on the map each team wants control,
what information each team most needs (Team B needs to know where Team A's
jungler is far more urgently than the reverse), which objectives are worth
contesting early versus late, and how urgently each side needs to convert
an advantage before its window closes. Assessment 3 asks you to do exactly
this analysis, on a real draft, before the match is played.
