## Guiding question

> When is taking Dragon the wrong decision?

## An objective existing is not a reason to take it

Dragon spawning, Herald being up, a tower being low — none of these are
instructions. Each is an opportunity a team can spend tempo contesting,
and like any other tempo spend (Week 6), it only pays off if what follows
is worth more than what it cost.

## The vocabulary

- **contest** — actively fighting for the objective right now
- **trade** — accepting the objective goes to the opponent, in exchange for
  something taken elsewhere: a tower, a pick, uncontested farm
- **concede** — giving the objective up without extracting a trade, because
  contesting costs more than it's worth
- **threaten** — presenting enough of a threat near the objective that the
  opponent can't safely take it either, without committing to taking it
  yourself
- **setup** — the positioning, vision (Week 7), and wave (Week 3) work done
  before a contestable window opens, so the choice below isn't made blind
- **objective access** — whether a team can safely reach the objective at
  all, independent of whether it wants to
- **opportunity cost** — what a team gives up elsewhere by spending this
  window here
- **conversion** — what the buff, gold, or tempo gained is actually spent
  on afterward

## Builds on Weeks 3, 5, 6 and 7 together

Whether an objective can be safely contested depends on wave states
elsewhere on the map (Week 3 — can everyone actually leave their lane?),
where the enemy jungler plausibly is (Week 5), whether there's tempo to
spend getting there and converting afterward (Week 6), and how confident
that whole read is (Week 7). Nothing here is a new mechanical concept; it's
where the previous four weeks' tools get used on a single call at once.

## An uncomfortable scenario

Dragon spawns in 20 seconds. Your team's situation:

- one player is mid-recall, having just taken a bad trade
- your support has placed no river wards in the last two minutes
- bot lane's wave is slow-pushing hard toward your own tower, unattended
- your jungler and one laner are already near the pit, but outnumbered if
  the enemy shows in full

Choose one, and be specific about what it buys and what it costs:

1. **Contest anyway** — buys a chance at the objective now; costs a fight
   taken outnumbered, on no information, with a teammate who can't help.
2. **Concede outright** — costs the buff and the gold entirely; buys
   nothing back, but avoids the fight and the wave loss both.
3. **Trade** — let the objective go, and instead let the pushing bot wave
   crash for free minions and tower plates, timed with the recalling
   player's return.
4. **Threaten without taking** — show up at the pit to deny an easy,
   uncontested take, without actually committing to the fight; costs
   nothing but the travel time, buys denial rather than possession.

There's no single correct answer graded here. What matters is that the
buy/cost pair named for the chosen option is accurate and complete — this
is the strongest demonstration in the course so far that League decisions
resist absolute rules.
